# moduleone

Normal's **team-health tool** — Module 1 of the Normal internal stack.

A weekly ~5-minute **360 check-in** (voice) scoring **Activity · Excellence · Morale**, transcribed and LLM-graded, rolled into a **Red/Yellow/Green heat-map** across teams and weeks. Built shallow + manual-input-first as a ~3-month stopgap.

## Stack
Next.js (App Router) · TypeScript · Tailwind + shadcn/ui · Supabase (Postgres + Auth, RLS) · Vercel. Reviewed by CodeRabbit (`.coderabbit.yaml`) and Qodo (`.pr_agent.toml`).

**The heat-map** is at `/portal/dashboard`. It opens on the **org chart**: a card per division, each domain and team inside it coloured R/Y/G for one week (this week by default; step back a week at a time), with bars for the eight weeks up to it. The **Trend** tab is teams × weeks (Singapore-time Mondays; the last 1, 4, 8 or 12, where a single week spells out each team's counts). Every box and cell is the mean score of that week's graded check-ins in the team and every team under it, and one that holds red check-ins says how many ("3 red"), since a green mean can hide them. A tally at the top right counts the week's teams by colour (a domain with teams under it counts through them; the organisation and divisions don't count). Open one to read the check-ins, reviews and transcripts, team by team, and play recordings if you may. It reads with the viewer's own login, so RLS decides what each person sees: hq the whole org, a leader the teams they lead (the division and domain above them show uncoloured, since they can't read those check-ins). Members don't see grades; they're sent to their Diary.

**The Diary** (`/portal/checkin`, "Diary" in the app bar) is each member's own page: this week's check-in at the top (record, listen back, submit), then "Then and now" (the earliest recording they can still play next to their latest) and their entries, newest first and grouped by year, each with a player and the date it's kept until. It never shows a score, theme, review or transcript. Recordings play through `/portal/dashboard/recording/[checkinId]`, which signs a ten-minute link as the viewer when they press play, so the page signs nothing for them as it loads (only this week's unsubmitted draft gets a link with the page, for its own player). There is no privacy notice in the app: members agree on the onboarding form, outside Module One (the product owner's decision of 13 October 2026), and the Diary notes that assumption for each login whenever it loads, and again before any recording is uploaded (`src/lib/checkin/onboarding-note.ts`). A submitted recording is kept for a year if its take started after that note, or after the member had accepted the old one-year in-app notice (and nothing shorter since); otherwise 90 days, as the notice they recorded under said (migration 0013). Files never submitted still go after 90 days.

**What members are told:** the onboarding form, outside Module One, is the only place members are told how their recordings are used, and it must stay true to what the app does: the app can't check it. Anything that changes the list below (a new speech-to-text service, the live check-in, who sees what, how long anything is kept) waits until the product owner has changed the form. This is what the last in-app notice said, and what the form must cover:

- **What is recorded:** only their voice, from Start to Finish. The camera's picture is never saved or sent. With `LIVE_CHECKIN=on`, their words are also turned into text as they speak, for follow-up questions.
- **Why:** a weekly picture of each team for their leader and HQ (what got done, where people used their strengths, how they feel about the team), and a diary they can listen back to.
- **Who processes it:** on submit, the recording goes to OpenAI in the US, or to the `STT_PROVIDER` and `STT_FALLBACK` services (for example a server Normal runs), to be turned into text; Anthropic in the US then scores it and writes a short summary. With `LIVE_CHECKIN=on`, audio streams to OpenAI in the US while they record, whatever `STT_PROVIDER` says, and the text so far goes to Anthropic in the US for the next question.
- **Who sees what:** their leader and HQ see the transcript, the scores and the summary; the member doesn't see their scores. The Diary shows only their own recordings. Only they and holders of the `recordings` grant can play a recording. A draft is theirs alone until they submit. With `LIVE_CHECKIN=on`, neither the live text nor the questions' wording is kept; a record of the topics asked, how much of each was covered and the mood (a hard week, or not coping) is kept with the check-in. It never holds their words, and nobody sees it in the app.
- **How long:** submitted recordings a year (ones made under the earlier 90-day notice, 90 days); takes never submitted 90 days; the live record of a take never submitted 14 days; transcripts and scores are kept as the team's check-in history.
- **Questions, access, correction or earlier deletion:** ask HQ.

**Tuning the heat-map:** the R/Y/G rules live in the single `scoring_settings` row: what each 1–5 score of activity, excellence and morale counts for, and the green and yellow thresholds. Members with the `admin` grant change them at `/admin/scoring`. Score values have two decimals and must be between 0.01 and 1000. Thresholds also have two decimals: yellow must be above 0 and green at most 999,999.99. The database refuses settings outside these limits or that leave a colour unreachable. Colours are computed from the stored scores on every page load, so an edit recolours past weeks too.

**Admin grants** (`member_grants`): `admin` edits teams, scoring and other members (not `hq` members, not themselves; only the project owner makes someone `hq`) and notes the email someone's first invite will go to; `recordings` plays anyone's submitted recording (never a draft); `big_five` reads and edits Big Five profiles. Only the project owner adds or removes grants, in the Supabase Table Editor, so no one can promote themselves through the app.

**The admin pages** (`/admin`, linked from the portal for holders of the `admin` grant): the team structure, the scoring settings, and a page per division, domain or team (open it from the structure) where admins add people (one at a time, or a pasted list with Add a list) and take them out of it, make them leaders or members, choose who else leads it, note the email each person's first invite will go to, give people a login (one at a time, or, with Send invites, everyone there with a noted email), change the email they sign in with, and remove them from Module One. The structure is a top-down chart (React Flow): drag a box onto another to move it there (a team into a domain, a domain into a division), or use its ⋯ menu's Move to…; click a box for the same people and leads controls in a side panel; "Show people" puts everyone on the chart beside their box, to drag between teams; "No team" lists everyone who sits nowhere, with the same login, invite email and removal controls. "Find a person or team" pans the chart to a match and opens its panel (a person's team, or No team); archived boxes and removed people aren't offered. Anyone can sit in a division, a domain or a team (since migration 0005, a division's head sits in the division itself, so their check-ins are the division's own), and a leader leads where they sit and everything under it, plus any node they're added as a lead of. Above every division is the organisation, The New Normal (migration 0006): someone who sits there (placed by the project owner in the Supabase dashboard, never by an admin) leads every division and sees every check-in in them. Whoever sits in a division or the organisation leads it: placing a member there makes them a leader, and they can't be made a member while they sit there. A node's title for its leaders (`leader_title`, "President" for the organisation) is a label admins can rename; it never changes what anyone sees. The app calls the `hq` role "Master Admin"; it has nothing to do with the HQ division.

**Grading model and costs:** each check-in is transcribed by OpenAI (`STT_MODEL`) and graded by Claude in one call that returns the scores, theme and review as JSON (`src/lib/grader`). The Claude model is `ANTHROPIC_MODEL` (default `claude-haiku-5-5`); set it to `claude-sonnet-5-5` in Vercel's environment variables to switch without a code change. The rubric is sent as a cached system prompt. Every successful transcription and grading call is logged in `processing_costs` (migration 0008: audio minutes, tokens, model; no transcript or member), and admins see the monthly totals, priced from `src/lib/costs`, at `/admin/costs`. Before switching models, compare them on real transcripts with `src/lib/grader/compare.live.test.ts` (instructions at the top of the file; its output holds members' transcripts and is git-ignored).

**Live check-in and the rubrics:** every check-in asks one open question, the opening question in `rubrics/coach.md` (if that file can't be used, a built-in one in the wording it was last given in code, today the same as `coach.md`'s, and no live coaching); the recorder no longer shows the three fixed questions. With `LIVE_CHECKIN=off` (the default) that question is the only one: no follow-ups, no live transcription, no coach calls. With `LIVE_CHECKIN=on`, follow-up questions also appear on screen while they talk, only when an area still needs one. While the member records, the browser streams a copy of the microphone straight to OpenAI's realtime transcription (`STT_LIVE_MODEL`, default `gpt-live-transcribe`, whatever `STT_PROVIDER` says) over a connection the server opens for that recording: the browser sends its WebRTC offer to the server, which opens one OpenAI transcription session per live session and sends OpenAI's answer back (`src/lib/stt/live.ts`), so no OpenAI key, not even a short-lived one, reaches the browser. At pauses the browser sends the text so far to the server, where Claude (`COACH_MODEL`, default `claude-haiku-5-5`) reads which topics they have covered and plain code (`src/lib/coach/policy.ts`) picks the follow-up question shown at their next pause. If anything live fails, follow-ups stop for the rest of that take: the recording carries on, the question on screen stays, and the recorder says once "Follow-up questions have stopped. Keep going, and press Finish when you're done." Either way the recording is uploaded, transcribed again and graded exactly as before, and the live text is neither graded nor kept. The three areas (activity, excellence, morale) are still what is graded, and the grader is still told about the three fixed questions, since check-ins recorded earlier answered them. What the grader scores and what the check-in asks are two plain files, `rubrics/grading.md` and `rubrics/coach.md`, that the prompts are built from: edit them on GitHub like any file ([`rubrics/README.md`](rubrics/README.md) explains both, the editing rules, the metrics and the tuning loop). Their tests check them on every PR, and every grade stores a fingerprint of the grading request that made it in `checkins.rubric_version`. The browser uses four routes, same-origin JSON POSTs that take the member from the session: `/portal/checkin/live` starts a session (live check-ins on, `coach.md` usable, not yet submitted this week, under 12 sessions in 24 hours), `/portal/checkin/live/connect` opens its live transcription (once per session: migration 0012's `connected_at`), `/portal/checkin/live/coach` takes the transcript so far and returns the next thing to show, and `/portal/checkin/live/end` counts the question or closing line on screen and ends the session. Vercel's logs get codes and counts only, never ids, topics, coverage or anything said: a line when a session starts (the coach's fingerprint and the models), a failed Claude read's code and HTTP status, and at each end why follow-ups stopped, if they did, with the session's counts (`rubrics/README.md` section 5). Migration 0011 adds `live_checkin_sessions`, one row per live recording, which only the server reads and writes (no grants to members or anyone else): topic ids, coverage levels, the mood and counts (`coach_state`), the models, the coach's fingerprint and how long live transcription ran, never the member's words or the questions' wording. It is linked to the check-in once submitted; the daily job deletes sessions never submitted after 14 days, and now also ends sessions the browser never ended, logging their live transcription. Switching `LIVE_CHECKIN` on changes where audio goes while members record and what is kept (`live_checkin_sessions`): the onboarding form must say so first; the app no longer asks members. On the portal home, the check-in tile says "A few minutes, starting with one question." and the "This week's question" tile always shows the opening question and, only while live check-ins are on, a note that follow-ups may appear. `/admin/costs` shows live minutes, live transcription, coach calls and coaching in their own columns, counted in the total and per check-in (a live session is paid for whether or not it was submitted).

### Before inviting
"Give login" emails an invite from Supabase Auth ("Send invites" sends one the same way to each person in turn), and the link brings the person back to this site signed in. Add a list and Set email (below) email nobody and need none of this. On the hosted project, the owner does this once before the first invite:

1. **Custom SMTP** (Authentication → Emails → SMTP Settings), and a higher email rate limit (Authentication → Rate Limits). Supabase's built-in sender only mails members of the Supabase organisation, at most 2 emails an hour for the whole project, so invites to anyone else fail ("Supabase can't email that address yet"). Steps in [#7](https://github.com/the-newnormal/moduleone/issues/7).
2. **Site URL and redirect URLs** (Authentication → URL Configuration): Site URL is the production address (the invite link is built from it), and the redirect URLs include `<production URL>/auth/callback**`.
3. **The invite template** (Authentication → Emails → Templates → Invite user): subject "Your Module One login", and paste the whole of `supabase/templates/invite.html` as the body. Supabase's default invite link doesn't work with `/auth/callback`. Its link expires with the email OTP expiry (1 hour by default).
4. **Vercel environment variables**, then redeploy: `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` (Supabase → Project Settings → API keys: the secret key, or the legacy `service_role` key) as a **server-only** variable, never with a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` prefix, marked Sensitive; and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL`, the production URL without a trailing slash, the same as the Site URL in step 2. Without them the page says logins can't be given, sign-in emails changed or people with a login removed yet.

Only the team page's `giveLogin`, `resendInvite`, `changeEmail` and `removePerson` actions (`src/app/admin/teams/[id]/actions.ts`), the check-in's server code (recording, transcribing and grading, tidying old drafts and files and the daily job `src/app/portal/checkin/housekeeping.ts`, the live check-in's session store `src/lib/checkin/live-sessions.ts` and the cost log `src/lib/costs/record.ts`, which writes with the client its caller passes) and the Master Admin's delete-recording and reset actions (`src/app/portal/dashboard/[teamId]/[week]/actions.ts`) use the service-role key, through `src/lib/supabase/admin.ts`; Add a list and Set email write as the signed-in admin. Give login records who gave each login and when, and never gives one to a Master Admin, to someone who already has a login, to someone who holds grants, or to a row that still has check-ins, a Big Five profile or recordings from an earlier login (a new login would inherit them; the project owner links those rows in the dashboard). It also refuses an address noted for someone else's invite.

**Adding a list, and noting emails before inviting:** **Add a list** (on the page or side panel of any division, domain or team that isn't archived) adds up to 200 new people at once. Type or paste one person per line: their name, then the email their invite will go to if you have it, and optionally Member or Leader where that person's role isn't the one picked for the whole list (everyone placed in a division leads it). Lines from a spreadsheet split at tabs, others at commas or semicolons outside double quotes (as a CSV file's do); what a mail program copies (`"Mei Wong" <mei@example.com>`, `mailto:`) works too, quotes and a bullet or number in front of a name are left out, and a first line of column headings is skipped. The dialog checks each line as it's typed and points out a name that matches someone already there, or someone Add people could move there instead. The server checks the list again, refuses an address that's on it twice or already noted for someone else, and adds it in one transaction as the signed-in admin (migration 0014's `admin_add_members`, so RLS and the members triggers decide each row as they do for one person): everyone on the list, or nobody. Nobody is emailed; their addresses are only noted. For anyone Give login is offered for, **Set email** on their row notes the address their invite will go to (**Edit email** changes or forgets it, but only while the note is still the one the page showed: if another admin changed it meanwhile, nothing changes and the dialog asks you to reload), and the row then says "Email noted for their invite"; again nobody is emailed. Noted addresses live in `pending_login_emails` (migration 0014), which only admins can read, not on `members`, which teammates read. No two people have the same one, an address that already has a login can't be noted (the dialog says which), no login is linked to anyone else, or given that address by Change email, while its address is noted for someone, and a person's is forgotten once they get a login, are removed from Module One, are made a Master Admin or are given a grant. **Give login** starts from the noted address (edit it there if needed). **Send invites (n)**, at the top of the People list whenever someone there has a noted address and no login, lists them with their addresses, then sends each one their invite in turn, exactly as Give login would, but only to the address still noted for them (if another admin has changed it since the page loaded, or changes it while the invite goes out, that person is skipped and any login made for the old address is deleted, so its link opens nothing: reload and check it), and says how many went and who didn't get one, and why. A refusal about one person (that address already has a login, records from an earlier login, a changed address, …) skips them and carries on; anything else, such as Supabase's email rate limit ("Too many emails were sent just now"), stops it there and names who it didn't get to: press it again later for the rest.

Each person's row says whether they can sign in yet: **No login yet**; **Invite not used** or **Invite expired**, with when the invite was sent; **Can't sign in yet** (a login made in the dashboard that was never invited or confirmed); **Never signed in** (a login set up ready to use, for someone who signs in at the login page); or **Active**, with the day they last signed in with a link (staying signed in doesn't update it). If the status can't be read, the row just says **Has a login**. The admin pages read this through `admin_login_states` (migration 0010), which gives admins each login's state and those times but never an email address. "Invite expired" assumes the email OTP expiry is still an hour. If you change it, change everything that says the link lasts an hour with it: `INVITE_LINK_MS` in `src/app/admin/teams/[id]/team-view.ts`, `supabase/templates/invite.html` and `magic-link.html` (then paste them into the dashboard again), "It expires in an hour" in `src/app/login/login-form.tsx`, and this README.

An invited person signs in from the invite link first. Until they do, `/login` can't send them a link (Supabase counts them as not signed up yet), and the link expires after an hour. If it expires or gets lost, an admin uses **Resend invite** on their row (offered until the invite is used, for logins given in Module One; the project owner re-sends the others with Authentication → Users → Invite user, at the same address): Supabase sends a new link to the same address, and the old one stops working. For a mistyped address, an admin uses **Change email**: the invite goes to the new address instead, and the old link stops working. Supabase also re-sends a pending invite when Give login is tried with an address another member was invited at and hasn't used yet: that member gets a new link (their old one stops working), and the admin is told "That email already has a login."

**Changing someone's sign-in email:** admins use **Change email** on their row, at any time (not for Master Admins, themselves, grant holders, or anyone who sits in or leads the organisation; the project owner changes those in Authentication → Users). If they've used their login (or it was set up ready to use), the address changes at once and nobody is emailed (tell them yourself; they stay signed in); if they're still waiting to use their invite, a new invite goes to the new address and the old link stops working. Their row says which admin changed it and when (`login_email_changed_by` / `_at`). Whoever reads the new address can sign in as them, so an admin could point someone's login at an address of their own: the project owner accepted that, and the row records it.

**Removing someone from Module One:** admins use **Remove from Module One** on their row (on a team page, the Structure side panel, or No team), with the same exceptions. They can't sign in any more (their login is deleted), they leave their team and everything they lead, and the admin pages stop listing them, while their check-ins and recordings stay, so past weeks on the heat-map don't change. The row stays in `members`, marked `removed_at` / `removed_by`; only someone who never had a login and left nothing behind is deleted outright (migration 0009's `admin_remove_member`). If cleaning up the login fails, the page says so: the login opens nothing, and the project owner finishes it from that row's `removed_login_id`: delete that user in Authentication → Users if it's still there, and clear `auth_user_id` on any member row that points at it. Only the project owner can bring a removed person back: in the Table Editor, clear `removed_at`, `removed_by` and `removed_login_id` together (if `removed_login_id` is set, first delete the user it names in Authentication → Users, unless a member row's `auth_user_id` is that user), then place them; for a row with check-ins, recordings or a Big Five profile, link a login in the dashboard, since Give login refuses those. To take away only a login, keeping the person, the project owner deletes the user in Authentication → Users; the row keeps its check-ins and recordings, so Give login won't link a new login to it.

## Rules
See `CLAUDE.md` for the engineering contract. Hygiene is non-negotiable: protected `main`, migrations-in-Git, RLS on every table, CI + required Supabase migration check + an approving review before merge (CodeRabbit's counts). Claude merges a PR once CodeRabbit approves it, Qodo's review leaves no open "Action required" finding and CI passes, all on its latest commit. **No direct production changes. No merging past red CI or unticked pre-merge steps.**

### Branch protection
`main` is protected by the GitHub ruleset in `.github/rulesets/protect-main.json`, with no bypass (admins included):
- changes land only through a PR with 1 approval; a new push dismisses earlier approvals, and all review threads must be resolved
- the `ci / check` and `ci / migrations` jobs must pass, on a branch that is up to date with `main`
- `ci / migrations` applies every file in `supabase/migrations/` to a fresh local Supabase, and fails if one errors, leaves a table without RLS, or breaks an RLS test in `supabase/tests/`. It then applies 0013 (the Diary's retention) on top of check-ins pointing at recordings of different ages (`scripts/check-diary-retention-upgrade.sh`), 0006 (the organisation) on top of people sitting in divisions, an archived division and check-ins (`scripts/check-organisation-upgrade.sh`), and 0003 (the team tree) on top of hand-made teams, members and check-ins (`scripts/check-team-tree-upgrade.sh`), as production gets them. Run it locally with `supabase db start && supabase db reset && scripts/check-migrations.sh && supabase test db`, then `supabase db reset --version 0012 --no-seed && scripts/check-diary-retention-upgrade.sh`, `supabase db reset --version 0005 --no-seed && scripts/check-organisation-upgrade.sh`, `supabase db reset --version 0002 --no-seed && scripts/check-team-tree-upgrade.sh && supabase db reset`. `db reset` rebuilds the local database from the migrations and deletes its data; without it, an edited migration that was already applied is not retested
- no force-pushes, no deleting `main`

A classic branch-protection rule on `main` also applies until it is removed (required `check` on an up-to-date branch, conversations resolved, admins included). GitHub enforces both, so changing the JSON alone can't loosen what the classic rule requires.

To change the rules, edit the JSON in a PR. Once it merges, a repo admin re-applies it:
```bash
scripts/protect-main.sh           # needs `gh` logged in as a repo admin; creates or updates the ruleset
```
Without the CLI, first-time setup only: Settings → Rules → Rulesets → New ruleset → Import a ruleset, then pick the JSON file (an import always creates a new ruleset). For later changes, edit the existing `protect-main` ruleset on that page.
